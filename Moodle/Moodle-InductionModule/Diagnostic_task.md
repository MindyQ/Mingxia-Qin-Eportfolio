# The Impact of AI in Education  
   Artificial Intelligence (AI) is considered one of the core branches of science and engineering (Russell, 2010). McCarthy (2007) explains that AI is the science and engineering that develops intelligent machines, such as intelligent computer programs, to complete tasks, especially those that require intelligence to process. AI is an intelligent machine that can perceive enormous information from the environment, automatically draw on accessible data, and respond rationally to solve tasks most efficiently and optimally. It is important to point out that acting rationally must incorporate acting humanly in the science of AI (Russell, 2010). Especially when coping with human-related tasks, one solution can hardly be applied to multiple scenarios due to the complexity of human behaviors. Observing human behavior and adapting to it plays an important part in developing artificial intelligence.  
  Many industries benefit from AI technology, for example, intelligent chatbots for customer service, autonomous driving in transportation, data analysis in finance, and so on. AI technologies also impact education greatly. AI changes the way teachers work. Some schools have attempted to use AI behavior analysis tools in the classroom to detect students' attention levels so teachers can make adjustments when teaching to increase students’ engagement. Generative AI, which can generate texts, images, videos, and more, plays an important role in helping teachers prepare lessons. Besides, AI reduces the time educators spend on administrative work, including using AI-powered automated grading systems and facial recognition for attendance (Evolve Digitas, 2024). The quality and efficiency of educators’ work increase dramatically with the implementation of AI in their daily work.   
  Moreover, AI has brought enormous changes to how students can learn. “AI has the potential to revolutionize the way we learn and teach, making it more personalized, engaging, and efficient” (Harry and Sayudin, 2023, p. 260). One of the key changes is that learning becomes more efficient. In a traditional classroom, implementing differentiation to address students’ various needs and interests is often time-consuming. This is because teachers often need to be responsible for a group of students at one time instead of offering one-to-one tutoring. Teachers’ levels and resources also impact the quality of differentiation approaches (Reis and Renzulli, 2018). AI can support teachers in differentiating and personalizing students’ learning experiences. “It provides a unique opportunity to personalize learning and reinvent our modern structures to address pervasive myths and challenges that limit student learning” (Craig and Kay, no date, p. 2). By using machine learning, AI can analyze students' learning patterns, diagnose weaknesses and strengths in the subject, and offer individual learning recommendations. It can create a unique learning path to maximize the learner's potential. Using adaptive learning platforms is an important approach where AI can support differentiation—for example, using IXL, an adaptive learning platform that supports math study in my previous school. It analyzes students’ learning status as they answer questions and then generates practice at different levels and on different topics based on their performance. What students learn is determined by their level rather than their grade, class, or even teachers. Besides, the traditional full learning cycle consists of teacher instruction, student learning, and teacher-led assessment, feedback, and making adjustments to teaching and learning again. Students’ progress relies greatly on teachers’ work to complete a learning cycle. AI-empowered interactive learning can provide instant responses to students’ inquiries and immediate feedback on their work. In this way, AI can shorten the complete learning cycle and quickly prepare students for the next learning cycle.     
  There are many potential ways to use AI to help students learn. A growing number of schools are investigating the application of artificial intelligence to develop innovative educational models. For example, Alpha Schools, a new type of school empowered by AI, has furthered exploration of using AI in education. In this school, students use AI programs to complete their academic learning with two hours of their school time and spend the rest of the time developing skills for life. Human teachers no longer deliver lessons; they are reassigned to do supporting work instead (Farrell, 2025). This educational model brings various benefits, such as students learning at their own pace through adaptive AI systems and receiving consistent and standardized feedback; teachers saving time from repetitive work and delivering lessons, so they gain more time to coach and support students in need; students’ engagement also increases greatly (Choi and Chang, 2026). Despite the benefits of using AI in education, it is important to note that AI has clear limits. Machine learning builds on what it knows; for aspects where AI has never encountered or is never trained, for example, creative thinking and critical thinking, or aspects which are too complex for AI to process currently, such as human emotions, AI might fail to make judgments precisely. Acting completely humanly will remain a profound challenge for the science of AI. Hence, more exploration on achieving the balance between AI and human teachers is still necessary.
